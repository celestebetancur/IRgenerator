Space. Cavity modes are solved analytically for the shape: a box (cosine modes), a sphere (zeros of the spherical Bessel derivative jl′, with 2l+1 degenerate modes summed), a cylinder or pipe (zeros of Jm′ × axial modes for closed, open or half-open ends), or a bottle (a cylinder plus a Helmholtz neck). Source and mic coupling come from the mode shapes. The T60 per octave comes from the wall material, the medium's absorption and V/S (Eyring).

    
Scale × multiplies every length. Mode frequencies divide by the factor and decay times grow with it, so a basketball × 300 rings like a dome. The medium sets the speed of sound: helium raises every mode, SF6 lowers them, and water or steel make the cavity mostly ultrasonic.

    
Medium and boundary. Each medium has a speed of sound, a density and its own absorption law: ISO air absorption for gases; for liquids, molten metals, lava, jelly and flesh, viscous absorption from the shear viscosity plus a power law in frequency. Bubbly water uses Wood's equation (1 % air gives about 120 m/s) and an absorption peak at the bubbles' Minnaert resonance. The wall-to-medium impedance ratio decides the boundary: a wall softer than the medium (rubber in water, glass around mercury) is pressure-release, a different exact mode set (zeros of jl and Jm, sine modes in a box). A wall that matches the medium lets the sound leave (stone around lava is almost anechoic). Transmission sets how much of that loss applies; wall thickness turns the wall into a thin, mass-law shell.

    
Diffuse tail. Above the highest synthesised mode (set by max modes), a velvet-noise tail takes over. It is level-matched to the modal density of the top modal octave.

    
Tuning. snap pulls every mode (cavity and shell) toward the nearest scale pitch. Snapped modes pile up on the same pitches, and a little detune makes them shimmer. replace discards the physical modes and rings only the scale (with partials). add layers both. The tonal imprint adds resonant peaks at the scale pitches to everything reverberant, including the diffuse tail, echoes and an imported IR.

    
Echoes are a recirculating flutter: auto = across the shape (a sphere's diameter or a pipe's length), note = one period of the root (a pitched comb). Dispersion delays the highs (+) or lows (−) on every pass, which gives spring-like chirps.

    
Motion makes the whole space glide (it expands or shrinks while ringing: + falls, − rises) and breathe (each mode wobbles with its own phase).

    
Imported IR takes the last IR from the standard view, or any file, and plays it slower by its scale factor. That gigantifies a real room the same way.
